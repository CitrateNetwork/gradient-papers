# ATIS: Let Cheap Analog Whisper "Skip This" Before the Expensive Math Runs — Screenplay

**LOGLINE:** Let cheap analog electronics whisper "skip this" before the expensive math runs.
**THE ONE IDEA:** Pre-filter the unimportant words (tokens) with tiny, low-power analog circuits, before the expensive digital attention math runs, to save energy.
**RUNTIME:** ~4:05 · **PALETTE ACCENT:** cyan · **GUIDE-MOTIF:** Pip (a single warm-amber node of light, silent, reactive)
**MATURITY:** Theoretical — a hardware research proposal at phase one. No prototype has been built. The honesty beat is central, stated early (Scene 1) and again at the catch (Scene 7).
**COLD-OPEN LINE:** "Before we start, one honest thing: this one is still just an idea on paper."

> Voice: neutral warm VO; Pip reacts, never speaks. Every ON SCREEN direction is written as motion so
> an animator or video model has clear verbs. Motifs reference the Series Bible §4 dictionary, plus this
> paper's signature motif: a sieve of light that drops dim motes before a gate. Visual grammar: warm
> flowing waves = analog; crisp cyan grid = digital. 16:9; 9:16 reflow notes where composition changes.

### SCENE 1 — [0:00–0:32] — "One Honest Thing First"  (HOOK + MATURITY)
MOTIF: substrate; Pip; honesty beat (Pip dims, "?")
ON SCREEN: black. A single warm-amber node (Pip) sparks in, softly dimmed, a small "?" of light beside it. A faint translucent blueprint of a device floats past — clearly a drawing, not a solid object — then dissolves into the indigo lattice.
PIP: dims gently, then steadies — sincere, not sad.
VO: "Before we start, one honest thing. This one is still just an idea on paper. No device has been built. It's a research proposal, and we'll come back to that. But it's a lovely idea, so let's walk through it."
SFX/MUSIC: low warm drone; a single soft chime.
SHOT NOTES: keep it intimate and candid; this frames the whole piece. (9:16: blueprint drifts vertically.)

### SCENE 2 — [0:32–1:02] — "The AI Reads Every Word"  (THE PROBLEM)
MOTIF: the-bad-thing (reading everything); determinism grid (digital, cyan)
ON SCREEN: a long row of glowing word-motes appears. Hot cyan lines shoot between them until every mote is tied to every other — a dense, heavy thicket. The grid glows and hums with effort. Most of the word-motes are actually dim; only a few glow bright.
PIP: hovers back from the heat, a little overwhelmed.
VO: "When an AI reads, it weighs every word against every other word. That is thorough, and it is expensive. Double the words, and roughly four times the work. It only gets worse the more you feed it. And here's the thing: most of those words barely matter to the answer. A comma, an 'and,' a name you already know. The machine still burns full energy weighing every single one."
SFX/MUSIC: a rising electrical hum; connecting-line 'zips'.
SHOT NOTES: let the thicket feel physically heavy. (9:16: row runs top-to-bottom, lines cross the middle.)

### SCENE 3 — [1:02–1:32] — "The Old Fix Comes Too Late"  (PROBLEM DEEPENS)
MOTIF: the paradox; honesty
ON SCREEN: a cyan digital sorter tries to sweep away the dim motes, but it sits at the far end of the heavy grid. The costly work has already run before the sorter can act. The dim motes arrive at the exit only after paying the full price.
PIP: tilts, puzzled, circles the sorter.
VO: "Now, today's tools do try to skip the dull words. But they decide what to skip only after the pricey math has already run. It's like paying for the whole meal, then being told which dishes you could have left off. The saving comes too late."
SFX/MUSIC: the hum sags; a small deflating tone.
SHOT NOTES: emphasize the sorter's late position at the far end. (9:16: sorter sits at the bottom.)

### SCENE 4 — [1:32–2:08] — "A Bouncer at the Door"  (THE IDEA + signature motif)
MOTIF: sieve of light (the turn); analog waves vs digital grid
ON SCREEN: at the very entrance, a soft warm-amber wave rises and forms a sieve, in front of the cyan grid. A stream of motes falls through it: dim motes drop away below, a few bright motes pass upward through a cyan gate. Warm flowing gradients on one side, crisp cyan grid on the other.
PIP: surges bright, spins once — the "understanding" pulse.
VO: "So here's the idea. Put a cheap doorman right at the entrance, before any of the pricey math begins. A bouncer who waves the obvious no-shows away before they ever reach the expensive line. It does a rough sort in analog, smooth electrical waves instead of crisp digital numbers. Think of a coarse sieve before the fine milling, or skimming a page before you read it word for word. It doesn't need to be exact. It just needs to catch the words that are clearly dull, and let the rest through to be judged properly."
SFX/MUSIC: the hum releases into a warm rising swell; a soft 'whoosh' as the sieve forms.
SHOT NOTES: hero beat. Sell the warm-flow versus cool-grid contrast hard. (9:16: sieve spans the width, motes fall through center.)

### SCENE 5 — [2:08–2:40] — "The Physics Does the Math for Free"  (HOW IT WORKS)
MOTIF: analog inner-product (currents summing); comparator gate (cyan)
ON SCREEN: many small warm streams of current flow into one shared node and simply add together into a single brighter pool — no machinery, the wires sum by nature. That pool meets a glowing cyan threshold line: bright enough passes up through the gate, too dim drops below. A clean yes-or-no.
PIP: hops from the summing pool to the gate, connecting them.
VO: "The clever part is that the analog circuits barely work at all. Little currents flow together and add themselves up on their own. The physics does the arithmetic for free. Then one cheap yes-or-no test: bright enough, keep it; too dim, drop it. No fancy readout, almost no power."
SFX/MUSIC: a warm shimmer as currents merge; soft 'tk … tk' gating clicks.
SHOT NOTES: the natural summing should feel effortless, almost liquid. (9:16: streams pour down into one pool, gate at bottom.)

### SCENE 6 — [2:40–3:08] — "Only the Bright Ones Get Through"  (THE PAYOFF)
MOTIF: sieve → gate → expensive machine shrinks
ON SCREEN: only a handful of bright motes pass through the cyan gate into the digital grid, which is now small and light instead of a heavy thicket. The camera pulls back to show the whole flow: warm sieve, cyan gate, a shrunken expensive machine beyond.
PIP: brightens, drifts through the now-airy grid with room to spare.
VO: "Now only the words that actually matter reach the costly machine. And with most of them skipped, the expensive math shrinks a lot. Skip most of the words, and the heaviest step shrinks by roughly the same share, and it compounds, layer after layer. Same answer, far less work. The doorman did the cheap part so the machine wouldn't have to."
SFX/MUSIC: the swell resolves warm and open; the heavy hum is gone.
SHOT NOTES: contrast this airy grid directly against Scene 2's thicket. (9:16: bright motes rise into a light grid above.)

### SCENE 7 — [3:08–3:44] — "The Honest Catch: A Toll on the Way In"  (THE CATCH)
MOTIF: honesty beat (Pip dims, "?"); the DAC bottleneck
ON SCREEN: the scene cools. At the entrance to the analog sieve, a glowing toll gate appears. Every digital mote must be converted into an analog wave to enter, and that conversion glows hot and costly. Pip dims; a small "?" of light hovers.
PIP: dims; the quiet, honest beat of the whole piece.
VO: "And here is the real catch, and we promised to come back to it. Turning digital numbers into analog signals is its own toll, paid at the door. That toll can cost more than the whole shortcut saves. Unless it's cleverly avoided, it could erase the benefit entirely. That's the hardest problem here, and it is not solved. Remember, none of this is built yet. It's a proposal at phase one."
SFX/MUSIC: music thins to a single sustained note; room to breathe.
SHOT NOTES: lower brightness; sincere, not scary. Hold on the toll gate. (9:16: toll gate centered, Pip dim beside it.)

### SCENE 8 — [3:44–4:05] — "Why It Could Matter, and Where It Sits"  (WHY + TIE + BUTTON)
MOTIF: token/SALT (energy saved); series tie; Pip travels; honesty about NAT
ON SCREEN: a faint camera-like analog sensor feeds waves straight into the sieve with no toll gate; a tiny device sips only a thin trickle of warm energy. Then Pip zips down a lattice edge toward a ghostly jellyfish nerve-net glowing in warm analog waves, while a separate labeled zone-map sits clearly apart, connected only by a faint dotted line.
PIP: settles, glows steadily, then darts off toward the next region.
VO: "But if that toll can be solved, and cameras are promising, because their signals are already analog, then tiny devices could think while barely sipping power. Nature does this too: a jellyfish sorts its signals in analog before it acts. One honest note to close: the network's built pieces do not build this. ATIS stands on its own, an idea we're being candid about, on the way to finding out if it's true."
SFX/MUSIC: a warm resolve; a single bright rising note as Pip departs.
SHOT NOTES: end card is light-only: the sieve, still dropping dim motes. Title fades: "The Gradient Papers · No. V · Theoretical". (9:16: Pip exits upward.)

## Producer notes
- **Total VO:** ~515 words ≈ 4:00–4:10 at a warm ~125 wpm. Trim one sentence from Scene 3 or Scene 6 for a tight 3:40.
- **Hero beats:** Scene 4 (the sieve forming — the turn) and Scene 6 (the payoff) are the moments to spend animation budget on. Scene 7 (the catch) is quiet by design; give it silence, not spectacle.
- **Honesty beat is non-negotiable and doubled** (Scenes 1 and 7): this paper is the series' most speculative entry, so candor is the spine, not a footnote. Never cut either. The maturity label appears in the cold open and the end card.
- **Visual grammar:** warm flowing waves = analog; crisp cyan grid = digital. Keep it consistent so viewers feel the difference between the cheap sieve and the expensive machine.
- **Music:** reuse the series' warm evolving theme; the electrical-hum build (S2), the 'whoosh' as the sieve forms (S4), and the single sustained note (S7 catch) are this piece's signature sound stamps.
- **Fact anchors:** analog pre-filtering before digital attention, the coarse-sieve/rough-sort relaxed-accuracy goal, currents summing by physics (Kirchhoff), binary keep/drop via comparator (no ADC), the DAC toll-booth bottleneck that can erase the benefit, and vision-transformer/analog-sensor bypass all trace to the ATIS paper (Sections 3–4). NAT (Paper XI) explicitly does NOT implement ATIS; the button keeps them apart on purpose. Do not introduce figures the paper doesn't support.
