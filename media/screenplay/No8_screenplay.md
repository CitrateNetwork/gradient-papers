# The BR1J Constitution: The Rulebook a Network Writes for Itself — Screenplay

**LOGLINE:** The rulebook a self-governing network writes for itself, and can rewrite.
**THE ONE IDEA:** On-chain governance where your vote comes from your track record of contribution, not just the tokens you hold, with a living amendment process so the community can change any rule.
**RUNTIME:** ~4:10 · **PALETTE ACCENT:** cyan/gold · **GUIDE-MOTIF:** Pip (a single warm-amber node of light, silent, reactive)
**COLD-OPEN LINE:** "A network run by strangers still needs a rulebook. So who gets to write it?"

> Voice: neutral warm VO; Pip reacts, never speaks. Every ON SCREEN direction is written as motion.
> Signature motif for this paper: a lattice that *votes and reshapes itself* (a parliament of light) and a
> save-point ring that can be *re-cast* along a cyan seam (an amendment). Legal-adjacent, not legal advice.
> Motifs reference the Series Bible §4 dictionary. 16:9; 9:16 reflow notes where the composition changes.

---

### SCENE 1 — [0:00–0:30] — "Who Writes the Rulebook?"  (HOOK)
MOTIF: substrate; Pip
ON SCREEN: a dark indigo lattice hums with scattered gold nodes, but there is no center of order. Pip drifts through it and tilts up toward an empty space where a shape should be.
PIP: drifts, tilts, brightens with a question — curious.
VO: "A network run by strangers still needs a rulebook. Who can spend the shared treasury. What the fees should be. What counts as breaking the rules, and what happens when someone does. So who gets to write those rules? And just as important, who gets to change them when they turn out to be wrong?"
SFX/MUSIC: low warm drone; an unresolved chord.
SHOT NOTES: slow drift with Pip; keep the frame open and unfinished. (9:16: empty space sits above Pip.)

### SCENE 2 — [0:30–1:05] — "Don't Buy a Vote. Earn It."  (THE IDEA)
MOTIF: token/SALT (misused) → contribution feeding the lattice
ON SCREEN: first, a few nodes swell huge by hoarding pools of gold light, tilting the whole lattice their way. Then that image dissolves: many ordinary nodes feed steady streams of gold into the structure, brightening as they work, and their brightness becomes their voice.
PIP: dims at the hoarding, then surges bright as the fair picture forms.
VO: "The easy answer is to let whoever holds the most tokens decide. But that is just rule by the wealthiest, and it quietly pushes out the people who keep the network running. Citrate's constitution does something else. You earn your say by showing up. The more you actually contribute to the network, the more your vote weighs. Think of it like a co-op, where the members who do the work own the decisions."
SFX/MUSIC: a heavy, lopsided tone releases into a warm rising chord.
SHOT NOTES: the dissolve from hoarding to earning is the thesis; make it feel like relief. (9:16: hoard tilts, then inflow rises from below.)

### SCENE 3 — [1:05–1:40] — "Three Ways to Count"  (HOW IT WORKS 1)
MOTIF: proof/verification cyan; composite weight
ON SCREEN: a single node's glow splits cleanly into three equal cyan-edged facets, then fuses back into one balanced light. Each facet pulses in turn to show what it measures.
PIP: hops to each facet as it lights, connecting them.
VO: "Your weight blends three things, counted equally. Have you been an honest, reliable part of consensus. Have you done useful work, adapters, inference, data. And do you actually show up to govern. Blending all three stops the richest, and the loudest, from taking over. And there's a quiet twist: when the AI model on your node does good work, that lifts your contribution, and so it lifts your voice in governance too."
SFX/MUSIC: three clean tonal steps that resolve into one chord.
SHOT NOTES: keep the three facets exactly equal in size and brightness; equality is the point.

### SCENE 4 — [1:40–2:20] — "A Proposal, Start to Finish"  (HOW IT WORKS 2)
MOTIF: routing/flow; consensus ring scaling (quorum)
ON SCREEN: a proposal-mote is released and travels a clear track: it pauses at a discussion node, then weighted votes pulse in from around a ring, then a cyan gate opens and the change executes into the lattice on its own. Behind it, three concentric rings widen to show bigger decisions needing broader agreement.
PIP: rides alongside the proposal-mote, watching the ring widen for the big vote.
VO: "Anyone eligible can propose a change. The network discusses, then votes, then waits a set time so anyone who disagrees can leave, and then the code runs the outcome automatically. Small tweaks need a small agreement. Changing consensus itself needs far more of the network to say yes."
SFX/MUSIC: a civic, procedural pulse; a clean gate-open tone at execution.
SHOT NOTES: the widening rings sell "quorum scales with the stakes." (9:16: proposal track runs top-to-bottom.)

### SCENE 5 — [2:20–2:55] — "The Ring You Can Re-Cast"  (HOW IT WORKS 3)
MOTIF: checkpoint (re-castable); amendment
ON SCREEN: a locked white save-point ring softens, opens along a glowing cyan seam, reshapes, and firmly re-closes in a new form, still solid. A rule has changed, but by the rules.
PIP: pulses once as the ring re-locks — a nod.
VO: "And here is the part that makes it a living document. Even the constitution itself can be amended. The save-point ring, the thing that usually can't be undone, can be reopened and re-cast, as long as a strong majority agrees. No rule is carved so deep the community can't reach it."
SFX/MUSIC: a soft unlock, a held breath, then a firm, warm re-lock.
SHOT NOTES: the cyan-seam reopening is this paper's signature image; give it room. (9:16: ring centered, seam vertical.)

### SCENE 6 — [2:55–3:25] — "The Founders Benefit, Too"  (THE CATCH 1)
MOTIF: honesty beat (Pip dims, "?")
ON SCREEN: the scene cools. Pip dims and a small "?" of light hovers. Two brighter founder-nodes rest inside the ring, and a faint cyan seam glows on the ring right beside them.
PIP: dims; the honest, quiet beat.
VO: "We should be straight about something. The people who wrote this constitution are also the founders of the network, and they benefit from how the rules are written. That's a real tension, and we say it out loud. The amendment power exists exactly so the community can change any rule, including the ones that favor the founders."
SFX/MUSIC: music thins to a single sustained note.
SHOT NOTES: lower brightness; sincere, not scary. The seam beside the founder-nodes is the visual promise.

### SCENE 7 — [3:25–3:50] — "Slow, and Still Aspirational"  (THE CATCH 2)
MOTIF: honesty beat (Pip dims, "?"); half-formed human-AI bond
ON SCREEN: the weighted votes pulse slowly around the ring, a patient tempo. A faint half-formed cyan bond between a warm human-node and a cool AI-node flickers, not yet solid.
PIP: stays dim; a second honest beat.
VO: "Two more honest notes. Governing this way is slower than letting one boss decide. Discussion, a vote, a waiting period, then execution, all of that takes time. That's the price of everyone having a real say. And the idea of humans and AI as partners in this network is a direction we're aiming at, not a thing we've proven. For now, every vote is still cast by a human; the AI does the work, but people keep the final say. This is a rulebook to build on, not a finished promise. And it's legal-adjacent, not legal advice."
SFX/MUSIC: the single note holds; space to breathe.
SHOT NOTES: keep the human-AI bond deliberately unfinished. Do not resolve it.

### SCENE 8 — [3:50–4:15] — "Rules You Can Rewrite, and What's Next"  (WHY + TIE + BUTTON)
MOTIF: consensus in concert; series tie; Pip travels
ON SCREEN: pull back to the whole lattice voting as one living parliament of light, gold contribution flowing in, cyan eligibility gates glowing, the ring breathing open and shut. Then Pip zips down an edge toward a faint teal-and-magenta region where a ghostly jellyfish nerve-net shimmers.
PIP: settles inside the ring, glows steadily, then darts off toward Paper IX.
VO: "Put it together and you get a co-op for a whole network. Bylaws the members write by earning their voice, run automatically on-chain, and can always be rewritten from within. Next, the strangest inspiration yet: a five-hundred-million-year-old jellyfish that already solved how to coordinate with no one in charge."
SFX/MUSIC: full warm resolve; a single bright rising note as Pip departs.
SHOT NOTES: end card is light-only: the steady ring with Pip inside, cyan seam glowing. Title fades: "The Gradient Papers · No. VIII". (9:16: Pip exits upward.)

## Producer notes
- **Total VO:** ~515 words ≈ 4:00–4:15 at a warm ~125 wpm. To tighten to 3:40, trim one sentence from Scene 4 or Scene 7.
- **Hero beats:** Scene 2 (the dissolve from hoarding to earning) and Scene 5 (the ring re-cast) are the two moments to spend animation budget on.
- **Honesty beats are non-negotiable** (Scenes 6 and 7): the founders-benefit admission is a point of integrity, and the "slow / aspirational" caveat keeps the series honest. Never cut them to save time.
- **Signature sound stamps:** reuse the series' "lock-thunk" for the ring re-lock in Scene 5, and the honesty-beat "single sustained note" for Scenes 6–7, so this piece sits inside the established sound world.
- **Fact anchors:** contribution-weighted three-factor voting, on-chain propose→vote→wait→execute, quorum scaling with the stakes, amendment by supermajority, membership+stake gating, and both honesty beats (founders benefit; slow + human-AI aspirational, §4.2 and §7) all trace to Paper VIII. No figures are introduced that the paper doesn't support. Legal-adjacent, not legal advice.
